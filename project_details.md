Details on KM
background
 (“COMPANY”) has established the Value Chain Optimization (“VCO”) Continuous Improvement (“CI”) initiative to strengthen retrospective evaluation, organizational learning, and decision quality across the downstream value chain. The CI initiative builds on prior refinery and regional backcasting efforts and is intended to help COMPANY learn from actual outcomes, evaluate high-impact decisions after execution, and apply those lessons to future planning, scheduling, optimization, and execution decisions.
COMPANY’s CI framework includes a decision lookback matrix, maturity-based assessment of decision points, defined governance modes, and a growing set of operational lookback and backcasting processes. These processes generate valuable findings, lessons learned, decision rationales, improvement opportunities, and recommendations. However, the long-term value of those insights depends on COMPANY’s ability to capture them consistently, preserve the context in which they were developed, govern their use, and make them discoverable for future decision makers.
The need for a Knowledge Management (“KM”) capability was identified during the Integrated Backcasting project, which developed an initial conceptual framework for incorporating lessons learned into future planning processes. That work established an important foundation, but the KM capability contemplated under this SOW is broader than Integrated Backcasting. It is intended to support the full CI portfolio, including lookback processes, post-audits, backcasting activities, recurring CI reporting, and expert knowledge held by COMPANY subject matter experts.
Today, retention and reuse of CI knowledge depends heavily on individual experience, informal follow-up, and knowledge held by specific team members. While individual findings may be addressed through point-by-point actions, broader lessons, recurring themes, decision context, and reusable guidance are not yet captured in a standardized or consistently discoverable manner. This creates risk that similar issues recur, institutional knowledge is lost through role change or attrition, and future decisions are made without full benefit of prior learning.
COMPANY has determined that establishing a CI-focused KM capability is the appropriate next step in maturing the CI initiative. This capability will provide a structured foundation for capturing, contextualizing, governing, and reusing CI knowledge. It will also establish the knowledge quality, taxonomy, governance, and technology direction needed to support future AI enablement, while preserving Company ownership of the underlying business context, decision logic, and operating model.
This Knowledge Management activity is therefore established as a distinct design and foundation effort within the Continuous Improvement initiative. It is informed by Integrated Backcasting, but it is not limited to Integrated Backcasting. The purpose of this SOW is to define the work required to establish the CI-wide KM framework, validate and reconcile existing KM requirements, recommend an appropriate technology direction, and define the roadmap for subsequent implementation phases.
OVERVIEW
COMPANY intends to establish a CI KM capability that systematically captures, contextualizes, governs, and enables the reuse of institutional knowledge generated through CI processes. The purpose of the capability is to help COMPANY convert CI findings, lessons learned, decision rationales, recommendations, and subject matter expert knowledge into structured, searchable, and reusable knowledge assets that can improve future planning, scheduling, optimization, and execution decisions.
COMPANY has chosen a phased, CI-first approach rather than design and implement a broad enterprise solution in a single effort. This SOW establishes the design foundation for the KM capability within the CI portfolio, with an initial focus on requirements reconciliation, knowledge contextualization, governance, technology direction, and implementation planning. The resulting framework should be scalable beyond CI over time, but this phase is intended to define the foundation before COMPANY proceeds with broader contextualization, platform implementation, piloting, or AI-enabled capabilities.
This SOW includes one primary workstream: Knowledge Contextualization Framework and Foundation Design. Under this workstream, Consultant will reconcile COMPANY’s existing KM-related requirements inputs, validate the consolidated requirements against the full CI scope, define the contextualization framework, recommend knowledge quality tiers and confidence indicators, develop the governance model, provide an industry-informed perspective, recommend a technology approach, and define a roadmap for subsequent phases.
The engagement is expected to produce a clear and actionable foundation for COMPANY’s CI KM capability. This includes a validated requirements baseline, a standardized framework for classifying and contextualizing CI knowledge, governance processes for maintaining quality and accountability, an initial technology direction that supports future AI enablement, and an implementation roadmap for applying the framework to CI knowledge assets and extending the capability over time.
This SOW is a design and foundation effort only. It does not include full-scale contextualization of existing CI knowledge assets, procurement or implementation of a production KM platform, deployment of AI agents or automated recommendation capabilities, or enterprise-wide rollout beyond the CI scope. Those activities may be considered in subsequent phases based on COMPANY approval of the framework, technology direction, roadmap, and business case.
PROJECT SCOPE
This SOW establishes the CI-focused design foundation for COMPANY’s KM capability. The scope is limited to one workstream: Knowledge Contextualization Framework and Foundation Design. The workstream will define how CI knowledge should be identified, structured, contextualized, governed, quality-rated, and made discoverable across the CI portfolio.
The scope is intended to build from COMPANY’s existing requirements and conceptual work rather than begin from a blank page. COMPANY has developed two primary requirements inputs: the VCO KM Functional Requirements Document and high-level requirements for an AI-enabled KM solution developed through the lens of Integrated Backcasting. Consultant shall reconcile these inputs into a single validated requirements baseline and assess where the requirements need to be generalized, refined, or expanded to support the broader CI portfolio.
The scope applies to CI knowledge generated or used across the full CI portfolio, including Integrated Backcasting, lookback processes, post-audits, recurring CI reporting, decision reviews, operational learnings, recommendations, and relevant subject matter expert knowledge. The scope is not limited to Integrated Backcasting, although Integrated Backcasting will serve as an important input and reference point for the KM framework.
The scope boundaries and exclusions for this design-and-foundation effort are described in the Out of Scope subsection below.
DETAILED SCOPE
Knowledge Contextualization Framework and Foundation Design.
Consultant shall perform the following services under this workstream:
	1	Requirements reconciliation and validation. Consultant shall compare the existing AI-enabled KM requirements against the VCO KM Functional Requirements Document and develop a traceability view showing aligned requirements, unique requirements, potential conflicts, gaps, and items requiring COMPANY decision. Consultant shall validate the consolidated requirements against the broader CI scope through discovery with COMPANY stakeholders, including CI managers, subject matter experts, and Digital stakeholders identified by COMPANY.
	2	CI knowledge source inventory and assessment. Consultant shall identify and assess CI knowledge sources and artifacts that may need to be captured, contextualized, governed, or made discoverable through the KM capability. These may include formal deliverables such as backcast reports, lookback decks, post-audits, recurring CI reports, decision documentation, and recommendations, as well as informal and semi-structured sources such as email summaries, meeting recordings, working notes, and subject matter expert knowledge not yet captured in formal documentation.
	3	Knowledge taxonomy and contextualization framework. Consultant shall define the standard structure for classifying and contextualizing CI knowledge. This shall include recommended naming conventions, categories, tags, metadata fields, domain structures, capture templates, and contextual attributes needed to support search, reuse, governance, and future AI enablement. The framework shall address structured and unstructured knowledge and shall include templates for documenting lessons learned, best practices, decision rationales, recommendations, and expert judgment.
	4	Knowledge quality tier and confidence model. Consultant shall define a quality tier and confidence model for CI knowledge. The model shall distinguish authoritative, reviewed, and approved sources from indicative or support sources. For example, formal monthly CI reports, approved backcast reports, and governed lookback outputs may be treated differently from email summaries, meeting recordings, working notes, or informal SME inputs. The model shall define how source type, review status, recency, business relevance, and approval status should influence confidence and reuse.
	5	Governance model and lifecycle design. Consultant shall define the governance approach for maintaining CI knowledge over time. This shall include recommended lifecycle states, review and approval workflows, ownership roles, versioning expectations, auditability, refresh triggers, archival rules, and escalation paths for disputed or outdated knowledge. The governance model shall clarify how content moves from draft or indicative knowledge to approved and reusable knowledge.
	6	Industry perspective. Consultant shall provide an industry-informed perspective on how downstream refining and comparable process industries approach KM, knowledge contextualization, quality tiering, governance, and AI-readiness. This perspective shall be used to inform COMPANY’s framework and roadmap, not to replace COMPANY’s ownership of business requirements, decision logic, or operating model.
	7	Technology approach and architecture direction. Consultant shall recommend a technology approach for the KM foundation based on the validated requirements and contextualization framework. The recommended approach shall assess relevant solution options and identify a scalable, AI-ready architecture direction. Consultant shall support COMPANY Digital review of the recommendation to confirm technical viability, alignment with COMPANY standards, and suitability for future AI enablement.
	8	Implementation roadmap. Consultant shall develop a sequenced roadmap for subsequent phases. The roadmap shall address contextualization of existing CI knowledge assets, platform stand-up, pilot execution, governance activation, expansion to additional functions, and potential future AI-enabled capabilities. The roadmap shall also identify major dependencies, required COMPANY decisions, resourcing considerations, and business process changes needed to sustain the KM capability.
The engagement shall conclude with a final approval milestone at which Consult presents the requirements reconciliation and gap assessment, KM contextualization framework, governance model, technology recommendation, and implementation roadmap for COMPANY review and approval.
EXPECTED BUSINESS BENEFITS
The scope of this SOW is expected to provide the following benefits:
	•	A single validated, CI-wide requirements baseline that consolidates COMPANY’s existing KM requirements work and identifies gaps requiring decision or further definition.
	•	A standardized framework for classifying, contextualizing, quality-rating, and governing CI knowledge before broader platform investment or large-scale knowledge conversion.
	•	A clearer understanding of CI knowledge sources, source quality, ownership, and readiness for reuse.
	•	A governance model that supports accountability, review, refresh, and controlled reuse of CI knowledge over time.
	•	An industry-informed point of view on KM practices relevant to downstream refining and comparable process industries.
	•	A scalable, AI-ready technology direction that supports future KM implementation while preserving COMPANY ownership of business context and decision logic.
	•	A practical implementation roadmap for moving from foundation design to future execution phases.
OUT OF SCOPE
Unless otherwise agreed to in writing by the Parties, the services described in this Section III represent the complete scope of services to be performed under this SOW. The following activities are outside the scope of this SOW:
	1	Application of the KM framework to existing CI knowledge assets. This SOW does not include full-scale contextualization, tagging, cleansing, migration, or conversion of existing CI knowledge assets. Those activities may be addressed in a subsequent phase based on the approved framework and implementation roadmap.
	2	Pilot execution or operational rollout. This SOW does not include execution of KM pilots, deployment of new capture processes into day-to-day operations, or ongoing operational support of the KM capability after the design foundation is complete.
	3	Technology procurement or production implementation. This SOW does not include procurement, licensing, configuration, integration, deployment, or production implementation of any KM software platform, data repository, search capability, workflow tool, reporting solution, or related technology. Consultant will recommend a technology approach and support COMPANY Digital review, but platform selection, procurement, and implementation decisions remain COMPANY responsibilities.
	4	Build or deployment of AI-enabled capabilities. This SOW does not include the design, build, testing, deployment, or operation of AI agents, AI-assisted reasoning, automated recommendations, generative search, autonomous workflows, or other agentic capabilities. The framework and technology direction should support future AI enablement, but AI capability delivery is outside the scope of this phase.
	5	Enterprise-wide KM rollout. This SOW does not include enterprise-wide implementation of the KM capability beyond the CI scope defined in this SOW. Extension to other functions, including planning, scheduling, refining, terminals, logistics, or other business areas, may be considered in subsequent phases.
	6	Creation of unrelated reports or deliverables. This SOW does not include development of reports, dashboards, analyses, or deliverables that are unrelated to the CI KM capability, except where such materials are expressly identified as deliverables under this SOW.
	7	Ongoing ownership, maintenance, or governance operations. This SOW does not include long-term ownership, administration, content stewardship, periodic review, or ongoing governance execution for the KM capability. Consultant shall define the recommended governance model, but operational ownership and ongoing governance will remain with COMPANY unless otherwise agreed in a future SOW or change order.
