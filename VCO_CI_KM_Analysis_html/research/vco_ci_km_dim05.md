> On documentation weakness: "The fundamental issue is that port events are still, in most operations, captured manually and transmitted as PDFs. ## 2. Key Findings (mapped to the six mission questions)

### Q1 — Inventory performance reviews (days of supply, tank utilization, working capital) & stockouts
- **"Days of supply" is the canonical inventory KPI**, defined by practitioners as stocks ÷ trailing-4-week average demand, tracked weekly from EIA data and benchmarked against 5-year seasonal ranges [^869^][^868^]. External monitors (US states via NASEO) use the same WPSR/PADD data plus prime-supplier reports with a 2–3 month lag — meaning public oversight runs materially behind internal data [^876^].
- **Working-capital lookbacks are a board-level consulting product**: EY benchmarks DIO/DSO/DPO/CCC across oil & gas, found downstream CCC deteriorated (+2 days in Canada FY16–18) while other segments improved, and sized trapped cash at US$200b (US) / CAD$230b (N. America) — with explicit advice to add CCC/DSO to *quarterly business reviews* [^760^][^883^]. Carrying-cost benchmarks of 20–30%/yr frame the tension: too-low inventory drives stockouts and expedited freight that "often exceed the savings" [^885^][^887^].
- **Stockouts are measured episodically, not continuously.** The UK 2021 crisis put hard numbers on dry-outs (BP out of main grades at ~1/3 of its stations; ≥50% of non-motorway stations dry per the PRA) [^511^]; the remedy was *ad hoc* — suspension of competition law to allow data-sharing and coordination [^511^]. In the US, stockout response is codified as regulatory exception (FMCSA HOS waivers, up to 48 states; the 2021 Colonial waiver covering 18 states) [^854^][^859^] rather than as a planned network capability. At retail level, run-out prevention is now a sold software feature (ATG + AI forecasting; Warren Rogers/Gravitate "eliminate runouts") [^808^][^805^].
- **Retail inventory accuracy itself is a chronic variance problem**: a global c-store chain ran ~490 unmatched BOL investigations/month (~5,000/yr) and reconciled only ~94% of deliveries before automation (→64% variance reduction, 99% accepted deliveries) [^798^][^807^]; a 10-site DFS POC found 66k litres/yr of hidden losses *within legal tolerance* [^804^]. EPA's "1% of throughput + 130 gal" monthly reconciliation threshold means traditional methods "catch these variances only in retrospect" [^805^].

### Q2 — Demurrage, berth delays, pipeline scheduling/batching, interface losses
- **Marine demurrage mechanics are standardized; the lookback discipline is not.** Worldscale allows 72 hrs SHINC laytime; demurrage is liquidated damages, no despatch in tankers [^767^]. Voyager stresses "the leverage is upstream" (pre-arrival coordination, real-time event capture, NOR monitoring, claim validation, **port-performance benchmarking of terminals/counterparties**) while noting "port events are still, in most operations, captured manually and transmitted as PDFs" [^768^]. AI claims tools claim 10–20% cost reduction and 80%+ processing-time cuts (500 claims/yr × $75k avg → $5.6m at 15%) [^773^].
- **Demurrage is someone's cost and someone else's revenue** — Stolt Tank Containers booked +$71.3m demurrage/ancillary revenue in 2022 from "bottlenecks throughout the supply chain," then saw it fall $34.8m as bottlenecks cleared [^Stolt^]. OGJ documents a refiner at $2m/yr demurrage from berth occupancy/loading-rate/product-availability/early-arrival causes, with jetty scheduling problems forcing sub-optimal refinery operation [^779^]. Demurrage rates cited: ~$25k/day example charter; ~26 KUSD/day for a 40kDWT chemical tanker [^768^][^766^].
- **Rail demurrage is structurally contentious**: STB's 2019 hearings and Demurrage Billing Requirements final rule were prompted by PSR-era complaints (shortened free time, bunching, demonstrably wrong invoices); shippers are now seeking "reciprocal demurrage" on railroads holding private tank cars >72 hrs [^772^][^769^][^780^].
- **Pipeline capacity is rationed, not optimized**, on US trunk lines: Colonial's tariff runs monthly nominations → Cap Freeze → allocation by Calculated Cycle Historical Allocation and new-shipper lottery, with a 44¢/bbl Capacity Allocation Fee and 25,000-bbl mainline batch minimums [^794^][^795^]. 
- **Interface/transmix losses are quantified and material**: $50k per interface reported; "many millions of dollars per year" per operator; JP3/Phillips 66 claim industry losses could be halved [^834^][^837^][^845^]. Typical gasoline–diesel interface = 500–2,000 bbl per 100 miles; minimum economic batch size 5,000–30,000 bbl; manual cut decisions add 200–500 bbl of error, automation cuts interface into primary tanks 40–60% [^831^]. Disposition economics: refinery return recovers 70–85%, third-party processing 30–50% of product value [^831^]; EPA regulates transmix handling under 40 CFR 80.84 [^840^][^842^]. Persistent "over-protection" (conservative cuts) is documented as the rational response to missing real-time measurement [^834^].

### Q3 — OTIF / delivery reliability; emergency moves & premium freight
- **Generic OTIF practice is mature** (95–98% retail / 85–95% manufacturing targets; Walmart 75%→87%; root-cause reason codes; "a miss visible immediately, not weeks later in a monthly report no one can reconstruct") [^839^][^121^], but **fuel-specific public OTIF data is scarce** — reliability becomes visible only in crisis metrics: flights cancelled (54 at OR Tambo), stations dry (≥50% UK), waivers issued [^790^][^511^][^859^].
- **Aviation reliability failures convert directly into premium logistics costs**: airlines tankering extra fuel into OR Tambo "which costs the airlines dearly"; Swiss/Lufthansa diverting to Namibia to refuel [^786^]. After the Natref fire (72% of regional supply), 121.1m litres had to be railed/pipelined from Durban in tranches [^793^].
- **Emergency moves are institutionalized as waivers, not as capability**: US HOS relief recurs every winter (2013–14 run-outs → Reliable Home Heating Act 2014; 48-state Jan 2025 waiver; Dec 2025 Marcus Hook propane declaration extended into Feb 2026) [^855^][^854^][^847^][^860^]. Freight-KPI practice flags tender-acceptance and accessorial charges (detention) as early-warning metrics [^877^].

### Q4 — Network utilization lookbacks
- **Terminals report utilization/occupancy formally at board level**: Vopak proportional occupancy 84–94% across 2018–2024 (H1 2024: 92%) [^814^][^822^]; Stolthaven utilisation 91.7% (2025) vs 97.4% (2022) [^Stolt^]. **Attribution caveat**: occupancy swings are driven by market structure (contango fills tanks; backwardation empties them) and events like IMO 2020 conversions — so utilization lookbacks must separate commercial from operational causes [^823^][^822^].
- **Operational KPI sets for terminal lookbacks are well-defined**: ener8's eight KPIs — turnaround time, vessel idle time, berth occupancy, terminal occupancy, throughput, average waiting time, pumping time, average loading speed per product — with benchmarking via tankterminals.com (berth occupation, visit duration, tank turns) [^762^][^766^].
- **Pipeline utilization improves over time but is constrained by batching complexity**: US pipeline utilization rose to ~6m ton-miles/mile by 2007; researchers note it "would probably have improved more markedly if fuel regulations had remained constant" (boutique fuels) [^836^]. Transmix handling itself consumes pipeline throughput [^834^].
- **Secondary distribution is a documented optimization gap**: an academic petrol-delivery case found current practice ~16% excess mileage and ~5% excess cost vs optimized routing [^235^]; trucking wait-time at terminals was severe enough to trigger Iowa's HOS waiver [^851^]; TAS vendors sell load-rack throughput and pre-load validation to cut driver wait [^850^][^857^].

### Q5 — Tooling & how reviews are run/documented
- **Layered tool landscape**: (a) planning/scheduling — AVEVA Unified Supply Chain (plan-vs-actual comparison, "fractured systems force teams to waste hours reconciling data"; refiners claim 2–7% margin gains; ADNOC $100m/yr, 80% faster decisions) [^737^][^806^]; (b) pipeline ops — Emerson PipelineManager batch tracking with RTTM, DRA management, scheduled-vs-actual history per batch, cross-team "data unification" [^108^][^787^]; (c) marine/berth — Dropboard, MISMARINE MTO, UAB-Online with time-stamped event logs built for demurrage defense and "audit-ready records," plus ML trained on thousands of historical port calls [^440^][^784^][^770^]; (d) terminal — DTN TAS and Varec FuelsManager: real-time inventory by tank/product/owner, automatic gain/loss allocation at close, close-out reports, book-vs-physical reconciliation [^850^][^857^]; (e) retail — Titan Cloud/DFS/Warren Rogers SIR & wetstock analytics [^798^][^804^][^808^]; (f) bunker — Ascenz Marorka with historical trend/benchmarking analytics [^863^].
- **Reviews are documented increasingly as by-products of execution systems** (event logs, close-out reports, audit-ready records) rather than as standalone retrospective documents [^784^][^857^]. External/consulting lookbacks run at quarterly/annual cadence (EY QBR recommendation; Stolt/Vopak annual KPIs) [^883^][^Stolt^][^814^].
- **World Bank CPPI 2025 quantifies the berth-waiting problem globally**: ~11 hours average from port-area arrival to all-fast; IMO's 2026 Port Call Optimization Guide (building on Just-In-Time arrival work) targets exactly this idle time [^799^].

### Q6 — Known weaknesses (evidence-backed)
1. **Data split across functions/systems persists despite decades of tooling.** AVEVA: "fractured refinery planning and scheduling systems force teams to waste hours reconciling data" [^737^]. Emerson sells "data unification across planning, scheduling, operations, and accounting" precisely because it doesn't exist natively [^108^]. Voyager: port events "captured manually and transmitted as PDFs" [^768^]. Titan: "massive spreadsheets and error-prone manual entries" [^807^]. KM studies: "information is scattered across disconnected systems" [^864^]; IHS: millions invested "yet their engineers and scientists still can't find the information they need when they need it" [^875^].
2. **Recurring bottlenecks not structurally fixed.** OR Tambo: authorities "have known of the inadequacies of OR Tambo's storage facilities for over a decade but have yet to take any decisive action"; the 2022 valve failure recurred in Dec 2024; backup bypass promised after 2022 was still being expedited in 2025 [^786^][^790^]. UK: 2017 resilience consultation identified reduced spare capacity; the draft Bill arrived 2021; powers only landed in the Energy Act 2023 — after the 2021 crisis had already hit [^873^][^871^].
3. **Lessons lost between seasons/teams.** RVP seasonal transitions recur every spring/fall yet still force emergency waivers (EPA May 2020 extension when winter fuel couldn't drain) [^821^][^830^]. Winter heating-fuel run-outs recurred 2013–14 → 2025 → 2025-26 despite a statutory playbook (Reliable Home Heating Act; NASEO guidance) [^855^][^860^]. KM literature attributes this to tacit knowledge leaving with rotating/retiring staff ("Big Crew Change"), lessons-learned databases that are "captured once and never updated," and tools that "sit outside daily workflows" [^864^][^875^].
4. **Misplaced incentives**: UK government found companies "do not see a commercial return in managing low probability, higher impact risks" — hence no private structural fix without regulation [^873^]. Demurrage-as-revenue (shipowners, logistics providers) vs demurrage-as-cost (charterers) blunts shared incentive to eliminate delays [^Stolt^][^768^].
5. **Visibility asymmetry between chain partners**: airlines "remain in the dark about stock levels" at OR Tambo because procurement sits with private suppliers [^785^]; government information powers (notifiable incidents, data requests) were created precisely because voluntary visibility failed [^865^].

## 3. Tensions & Caveats (cross-cutting)
- **Vendor numbers are marketing-grade.** AVEVA 2–7%, Emerson/DTN claims, Titan 64%, Mercora 10–20%, terminal-automation "23% throughput" [^858^] are vendor-reported, not independently audited; treat as direction, not benchmark.
- **Just-in-case vs just-in-time is unresolved**: working-capital programs push DIO down [^760^] while resilience reviews blame reduced spare capacity for fragility [^873^] — the same inventory is simultaneously "trapped cash" and "resilience buffer."
- **Utilization KPIs conflate market and operations** (contango occupancy) [^823^]; demurrage benchmarks conflate charterer fault vs port congestion [^768^].
- **Measurement gaps drive conservative behavior**: "over-protective" pipeline cuts [^834^], safety stocks "sized for worst-case scenarios" [^753^], and HOS-waiver dependence [^855^] are all rational responses to missing real-time data — which is the core CI/KM problem.
- **Public OTIF data for fuel delivery is essentially absent**; reliability only surfaces in failure (stations dry, flights cancelled, waivers). This asymmetry means lookbacks are strongest where tooling auto-captures events (berth, TAS, wetstock) and weakest at the B2B customer interface.
